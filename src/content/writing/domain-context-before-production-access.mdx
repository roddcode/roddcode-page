The ticket said: "delete user, laptop reallocated." I was 18, three months into my first dev job at a logistics company in Peru. I opened Active Directory, right-clicked, and hit delete.

What I didn't know at the time: this user's Exchange mailbox contained email threads tied to active import operations. In Peru, SUNAT — the tax authority — can audit those operations years after they close. If an auditor requests correspondence and the mailbox has been purged, the company has no legal defense. The emails aren't just communication. They're fiscal evidence.

I wasn't fired. What stayed with me was realizing that a standard IT operation, one I performed correctly and by the book, could have created a legal liability for the company.

## Production access without domain context

I had production access to a system without understanding the domain it operated in. It was a process gap rather than anyone's mistake: the instructions told me how to delete a user, and nothing told me what the mailbox held.

The same gap shows up wherever production access is granted without domain context. A developer with database access may not know that `DELETE FROM users WHERE id = X` cascades to invoices. An SRE can push config without knowing which endpoints serve PII, and a support agent can reset a password for the account that handles payroll. In each case the operator has the ability to perform the action and no visibility into what it costs outside the system.

## What I do now

I don't assume the operator knows the domain. I build the domain knowledge into the process.

```typescript
type ProductionAction = {
  type: string;
  description: string;
  domainImpact: string[];
  requiredApprovals: string[];
  reversible: boolean;
};

const actions: Record<string, ProductionAction> = {
  "ad:delete_user": {
    type: "identity",
    description: "Remove user from Active Directory and all linked systems",
    domainImpact: ["mailbox retention", "fiscal audit trail", "system access"],
    requiredApprovals: ["hr", "legal"],
    reversible: false,
  },
  "ad:disable_user": {
    type: "identity",
    description: "Revoke access without removing account or data",
    domainImpact: [],
    requiredApprovals: ["hr"],
    reversible: true,
  },
};
```

It's a mapping table, and its only job is to force a question most systems never ask: what are the consequences of this action outside of this system?

Before a production operation runs, it checks its `domainImpact` against a registry of active compliance requirements. If the operation touches a domain with fiscal implications, it waits for legal approval.

## What I ask now

Technical competence and domain understanding are different things. You can be excellent at Active Directory and still create a legal liability for the company if you don't know what the data represents outside it.

When I interview now, I don't ask about frameworks. I ask: *"tell me about a time you made a production change and later realized you didn't understand the business impact."* The answer tells me more about how someone works than a coding exercise does.


I made that mistake at 18. The change afterward was not extra caution; it was refusing to depend on the operator remembering the context. Before I touch anything in production now, I ask whether disabling the account would do the job instead of deleting it. It usually would.
